01

Waqf signs علامات الوقف

All five are defined on a single folio — ث, PDF page 630 — under the heading عَلَامَاتُ الوَقْفِ. This mushaf uses only these five. Notably it does not use لا (U+06D9), which appears in many Indo-Pak and Egyptian editions.

ـۘU+06D8

Compulsory stop

الوَقْفُ اللَّازِمُ — م

A small initial-form م. Stopping is required: continuing across it would join two clauses whose sense contradicts the meaning intended.

إِنَّمَا يَسْتَجِيبُ ٱلَّذِينَ يَسْمَعُونَ ۘ وَٱلْمَوْتَىٰ يَبْعَثُهُمُ ٱللَّهُal-Anʿām 6:36 — the appendix's own example

folio ثappendix p. 630
ـۗU+06D7

Stop preferred

الوَقْفُ الجَائِزُ مَعَ كَوْنِ الوَقْفِ أَوْلَىٰ — قلى

Ligature of قلى (for الوقف أولى). Either is permitted, but stopping is the better choice.

وَتَطْمَئِنُّ قُلُوبُهُم بِذِكْرِ ٱللَّهِ ۗ أَلَا بِذِكْرِ ٱللَّهِ تَطْمَئِنُّ ٱلْقُلُوبُal-Raʿd 13:28

ـۚU+06DA

Permissible stop, both equal

الوَقْفُ الجَائِزُ جَوَازًا مُسْتَوِيَ الطَّرَفَيْنِ — ج

A small high ج. Stopping and continuing are equally sound; the reciter chooses.

Continuing preferred

الوَقْفُ الجَائِزُ مَعَ كَوْنِ الوَصْلِ أَوْلَىٰ — صلى

Ligature of صلى (for الوصل أولى). A stop is allowed, but carrying on is better.

Embracing waqf

تَعَانُقُ الوَقْفِ (المُعَانَقَة) — ∴ ∴

Three dots, and the only sign that always comes in pairs: two candidate stopping points where stopping at one forbids stopping at the other. Never stop at both, never at neither.

02

Ḍabṭ marks اصطلاحات الضبط

The tashkil system, defined across folios و to ف (PDF 613–624) under the heading اصطلاحاتُ الضَّبْطِ. The governing idea is stated on folio ي: classically these marks were printed in red, yellow and green; early presses could not manage it, so the mushaf keeps everything black and distinguishes added letters by reducing their size instead.

Silent and conditionally silent letters

ٱرْكَبُوا۟U+06DF

Round zero — letter never pronounced

الصِّفْرُ المُسْتَدِيرُ — دائرة خالية الوسط

A small hollow circle above one of the three weak letters ا و ي. It marks a letter that exists in the RasmUthmani rasm but is never uttered — neither when continuing nor when stopping.

By far the most common mark in the book: 3,988 occurrences across the Quran.

ءَامَنُوا۟   يَتْلُوا۟ صُحُفًا   أُو۟لَٰٓئِكَ   بِأَييْدٍappendix examples, folio و

folio وappendix p. 6133,988×
أَنَا۠U+06E0

Upright rectangular zero — silent only in continuation

الصِّفْرُ المُسْتَطِيلُ القَائِمُ

A small upright hollow oval over an alif that is followed by a vowelled letter. The alif drops when you continue and is pronounced when you stop on it.

Rare and highly concentrated: 66 ayahs, almost all of them the word أَنَا۠. The five that are not: لَّٰكِنَّا۠ (18:38), ٱلظُّنُونَا۠ (33:10), ٱلرَّسُولَا۠ (33:66), ٱلسَّبِيلَا۠ (33:67), قَوَارِيرَا۠ (76:15).

أَنَا۠ خَيْرٌ مِّنْهُ   لَّٰكِنَّا۠ هُوَ ٱللَّهُ رَبِّىappendix examples, folio و

folio وappendix p. 61366 ayahs
أَنَا ٱلنَّذِيرُno mark

The deliberate blank

إهمالُ الأَلِفِ الَّتِي بَعْدَهَا سَاكِنٌ

Worth listing as a sign in its own right: where the same alif is followed by a sākin letter, the mushaf leaves it completely bare — no circle, no oval. Its rule is identical to the marked case; the mark is omitted because the alif necessarily drops before a sākin anyway. The absence is the convention, and the appendix says so explicitly.

Sukūn, idgham and nasalisation

مِنْ خَيْرٍU+0652 / U+06E1

Sukūn — small headless khāʾ

رَأْسُ خَاءٍ صَغِيرَةٍ بِدُونِ نُقْطَةٍ

The mushaf's sukūn is not a circle but the head of a dotless khāʾ ح. It marks a letter that is both quiescent and clearly articulated (مُظْهَر) — the tongue strikes it.

أَوَعَظْتَ   قَدْ سَمِعَ   نَضِجَتْ جُلُودُهُم   وَٱنصَرَفْنَاappendix examples, folio ز

folio زappendix p. 614
مِن لَّدُنْهُbare + shaddah

Complete idgham

تَعْرِيَةُ الحَرْفِ مَعَ تَشْدِيدِ التَّالِي — إدغام كامل

The first letter is stripped of any sukūn and the next carries a shaddah. Read: the first letter vanishes entirely into the second, losing both its body and its qualities. The shaddah signals the idgham; the bareness signals that it is total.

مِّن لِّينَةٍ   مِّن رَّبِّكَ   مِّن نُّورٍ   وَقَالَت طَّآئِفَةٌappendix examples, folio ز

مَن يَقُولُbare, no shaddah

Incomplete idgham / ikhfāʾ

تَعْرِيَةٌ مَعَ عَدَمِ تَشْدِيدِ التَّالِي — إدغام ناقص أو إخفاء

Bare letter, and no shaddah on the next. The letter's body goes but its quality (ghunnah, iṭbāq) survives — or, in the ikhfāʾ cases, it is neither cleanly articulated nor assimilated. Covers both oral ikhfāʾ (مِن تَحْتِهَا) and labial ikhfāʾ of mīm before bāʾ (جَآءَهُم بِٱلْحَقِّ).

U+064B–U+064D

Tanwīn — the shape is the rule

تركيب الحركتين وتتابعهما

A tanwīn is always two marks, and this mushaf draws them in two different arrangements. Stacked (تركيب) means iẓhār; staggered (تتابع) means idghām or ikhfāʾ. Same letter, same vowel, same codepoint — different picture, different ruling.

Each of the three tanwīns has its own pair of shapes, and they are not equally obvious. Section 03 shows all three from the printed page.

folios ح، طappendix pp. 615–616see §03
مِنۢ بَعْدِU+06E2 · U+06ED

Small mīm — iqlāb

المِيمُ الصَّغِيرَةُ — الإقلاب

A miniature م replacing the second mark of a tanwīn, or sitting over a sākin nūn, with no shaddah on the following bāʾ. It means the nūn sound turns into a mīm.

Two encodings, chosen by position: U+06E2 above the line (510 occurrences), U+06ED below it when the tanwīn is a kasratān (99 occurrences, e.g. مِن مَّكَانٍۭ بَعِيدٍ).

Small letters and the RasmUthmani rasm

ذَٰلِكَ ٱلْكِتَٰبُU+0670 et al.

Miniature letters — restored from the rasm

الحُرُوفُ الصَّغِيرَةُ

Letters left out of the RasmUthmani skeleton but obligatory in pronunciation are supplied in miniature. The dagger alif U+0670 alone accounts for 9,726 of them. The appendix's own examples: ذَٰلِكَ ٱلْكِتَٰبُ, دَاوُۥدُ, يَلْوُۥنَ أَلْسِنَتَهُم, يُحْىِۦ وَيُمِيتُ, بَصِيرًۢا, إِلَٰهِكُم.

Folio ي explains the sizing: scholars used to insert these in red at full size (and the fuller classical system used yellow and green too); since early presses could not print colour, size took over the job of distinguishing an added letter from an original one.

Small letter overrides the written substitute

الحَرْفُ المَتْرُوكُ لَهُ بَدَلٌ فِي الكِتَابَةِ

Where the rasm writes a substitute letter for the omitted one, pronunciation follows the miniature letter, not what is written. So ٱلصَّلَوٰةَ is read with an alif although a wāw is drawn.

Hamzat al-waṣl — small ṣād head

رَأْسُ صَادٍ صَغِيرَةٍ فَوْقَ أَلِفِ الوَصْلِ

The head of a miniature ص (for صِلَة) over the alif marks a connecting hamzah, dropped whenever the word is reached in continuation. 13,483 occurrences — the third most common special character in the text after the vowel marks.

لَهُۥ   بِهِۦU+06E5 · U+06E6

Ṣilat hāʾ al-ḍamīr — small wāw and small yāʾ

صِلَةُ هَاءِ الضَّمِيرِ — واوٌ صغيرة / ياءٌ صغيرة مردودة

After the third-person singular pronoun ه: a miniature wāw when it carries a ḍammah (U+06E5, 1,257×), a back-turned miniature yāʾ when it carries a kasrah (U+06E6, 995×). Both are two-count natural madd — unless a hamzah follows, in which case they take the madd sign and stretch to four or five counts.

The rule holds only in continuation, and only when the letters on both sides of the hāʾ are vowelled. Ḥafṣ has four departures from it — see single-place marks.

Madd sign

عَلَامَةُ المَدِّ — ~

Placed over a letter to require a lengthening beyond the natural two counts. The exact duration is a tajwīd matter, not a ḍabṭ one, and the appendix says so. 5,376 occurrences.

Folio ل also records a glyph this mushaf deliberately refuses: the madd is never used over an alif to stand in for a dropped alif. Writing آمَنُوا۟ that way is called an error found in some other mushafs — here it is always ءَامَنُوا۟, hamzah plus alif.

03

Tanwīn: one sound, two shapes تركيب الحركتين وتتابعهما

This is the one place in the mushaf where the arrangement of an ordinary vowel mark carries a tajwīd ruling. Folio ح (PDF 615) sets it out and prints both arrangements as specimens, in brackets, for all three tanwīns at once. Everything below is photographed from the mushaf itself.

The ḍammatān — where the difference is unmistakable

Because a ḍammah is a closed loop with a tail, doubling it two ways produces two completely different pictures. This is the pair to learn first; once you can see it here, the fatḥatān and kasratān follow.

All three tanwīns, as printed in the Quran text

The letter that follows the tanwīn, and nothing else. This is the rule a validator can check mechanically.

The encoding does not carry this distinction

Both configurations are written with the same characters — U+064B fatḥatān, U+064C ḍammatān, U+064D kasratān. A census of the full RasmUthmani text turns up 3,741 / 2,519 / 2,633 of them and nothing else: stacked versus staggered exists only in the glyph the font selects from context.

Unicode does provide separate characters for the open, successive forms — U+08F0 Arabic Open TanwinAlFath, U+08F1 Arabic Open TanwinAlDamm, U+08F2 Arabic Open TanwinAlKasr — but the mainstream RasmUthmani corpora do not use them. So: if your font's contextual rules are wrong or absent, the text is still "correct" by every codepoint check and silently misprints the ruling. Worth an explicit render test rather than a string comparison.

The same caution applies in reverse when ingesting text from elsewhere: a source that does use U+08F0–U+08F2 will not match a corpus that uses U+064B–U+064D, and normalising one to the other throws the distinction away.

04

Structural marks علامات البنية

Signs that organise the text rather than describe its pronunciation. All four are defined on folios ن and س (PDF 621–622).

١U+06DD

Ayah medallion

الدَّائِرَةُ المُحَلَّاةُ الَّتِي فِي جَوْفِهَا رَقْمٌ

An ornamented disc with the ayah number inside. The shape marks the end of a ayah; the number counts it within its surah. The appendix is emphatic that it may never be placed before the ayah — a rule worth encoding as a validation check.

In print: a pale-green disc with a thin dark rim, green Arabic-Indic digits, and a small pink floret above and below.

إِنَّآ أَعْطَيْنَٰكَ ٱلْكَوْثَرَ ۝١ فَصَلِّ لِرَبِّكَ وَٱنْحَرْ ۝٢appendix example, folio ن

۞U+06DE

Juzʾ / ḥizb divider

عَلَامَةُ بِدَايَةِ الأَجْزَاءِ وَالأَحْزَابِ وَأَنْصَافِهَا وَأَرْبَاعِهَا

Printed as a small star ٭ set inline in the text, marking the start of a juzʾ, a ḥizb, or a half or quarter of one. It is paired with a named medallion in the outer margin (الحزب ٢٣, ربع الحزب, and so on) — the inline star gives the exact word, the margin ornament gives the label.

۩U+06E9

Sajdah — overline plus miḥrāb

خَطٌّ أُفُقِيٌّ فَوْقَ الكَلِمَةِ + عَلَامَةُ المِحْرَابِ

Two separate marks working as a pair. A horizontal rule drawn over a word identifies what makes sajdah due (مُوجِبُ السَّجْدَة); a small dome/miḥrāb glyph after a word marks the point at which you actually prostrate. A larger miḥrāb medallion labelled سَجْدَة also hangs in the outer margin.

The two are frequently on different ayahs — in al-Isrāʾ the overline sits at 17:107 and the glyph at 17:109, two lines apart. All 15 places are tabulated below.

Appendix typography

Two quoting conventions run through folios و–ث and are worth preserving in any transcription: ornate brackets ﴿ ﴾, each with a small rosette flourish at the tip, enclose Quranic text; guillemets « » enclose the signs themselves and book titles. Three display headings are set in the same calligraphic hand: اصطلاحات الضبط (folio و), تنبيهات (folio ق), علامات الوقف (folio ث).

Two things a parser gets wrong here

The two marks are not always on the same ayah. In four cases — al-Naḥl, al-Isrāʾ, Naml, Fuṣṣilat — the overline sits on the ayah containing the س-ج-د root and the glyph sits at the end of a later ayah, up to two lines away. Anchoring the sajdah to the overlined ayah gives the wrong ayah number.

The margin ornament is sometimes shared. On mushaf pages 272, 309, 334 and 379 the sajdah medallion is merged into the hizb medallion rather than drawn separately. On page 454 they are two distinct ornaments in the same margin.

Five traps

1. U+06DC does two unrelated jobs. It is both the sīn placed above a ṣād to signal an alternative letter reading (2 places) and the saktah marker (5 places). Same codepoint, incompatible semantics — you cannot classify it without looking at the base character.

2. Unicode names contradict the printed forms. U+06EA is named "Empty Centre Low Stop", but the appendix specifies the imālah dot as نقطة كبيرة مطموسة — a large filled dot — and that is how it prints. Do not choose a glyph by its Unicode name.

3. Many RasmUthmani datasets silently drop the waqf signs. The widely circulated Tanzil "rasm_uthmani" text contains zero U+06D6–U+06DB characters. If waqf marks matter to your product, verify they are present before building on a corpus.

4. The tanwīn's shape is not in the encoding. Stacked and staggered share one codepoint each. A codepoint-level diff will call two texts identical while one of them prints the wrong tajwīd ruling — see section 03.

5. The sajdah has two anchors, not one. See the sajdah table — the overline and the glyph diverge in four of fifteen cases.
