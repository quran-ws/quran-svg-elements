## THE DOMINANT SPECIES — two, and they explain nearly all of it

**1. FUSED PAIR.** Two slash strokes drawn touching share ONE mark element
and therefore count ONCE. The word flags a deficit; the "missing" stroke is
right there, inside the element its partner owns. Found on p126 (e458),
p218 (e607), p298 (e880), p303 (e73), p337 (e815), p380, p385 (e292),
p459 (e312), p552 (e180). Fixed by contour-level override: a key matching
ONE contour's transformed bbox splits it out. **No ink is ever cut** — the
contours are already separate; only their grouping changes.

**2. NAME INVERSION.** fatha and kasra are the SAME STROKE, named by
position relative to the word that holds it. So a mis-owned stroke always
arrives wearing the OPPOSITE name — a stolen fatha hangs below its thief's
letters and is called "kasra", and vice versa. This is why Abdullah kept
asking "why is the fatha at the bottom and the kasra on top": the wrong name
is the SYMPTOM of the wrong owner, not a second defect. Re-seat the stroke
and the name follows from position again.

**The ظ-loop trap (p218, cost one wrong fix):** a compound letter path's
contours include its WHITE HOLES. A hole is slash-sized and looks like a
candidate stroke; splitting it out painted the hole black (217 bad pixels).
Any contour-level rule MUST reject a contour nested inside another one, and
must never touch body elements. Rule: `audit_pixels` is what caught it —
never trust a split without it.
